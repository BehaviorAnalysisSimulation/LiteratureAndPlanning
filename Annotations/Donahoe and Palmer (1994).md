# A Biobehavioral Approach

The reference in [Donahoe and Palmer (1989)](../References.md#donahoe-palmer-1989) to the "in press" version was:
- Donahoe, J. W., Palmer, D. C., & **Carlson, N. C.** (in press). *Complex Human behavior* ***: A biobehavioral approach***. Boston: Ally & Bacon.

The final title when published in 1994 dropped one author, Carlson, and the subtitle, "A biobehavioral approach". I can see an author bailing out, especially on a book that was in press in 1989 but which was not published until 1994. But why drop the mention of the biobehavioral approach.

The first sentence in the preface:

>This book draws upon basic biobehavioral processes to provide a conceptually coherent approach to understanding complex human behavior.

I would recommend reading the previous Donahoe, et al., papers before this book.

# Behavior Analysis Basics

This book is a pretty good intro to behavior analysis. The complication in using it as an intro book is the incorporation of neuroscience/physiology as related to biobehaviorism. Skinner's works might be an easier intro to basics.

But, for anyone familiar with the basics of behavior analysis, EAB, JEAB, this seems to be a fairly painless intro to biobehaviorism.

# Neural Networks & Neuroscience

There are comments throughout regarding using neural networks in the formal interpretation behavioral and biobehavioral science:
1. See glossary entry "formal interpretation" below.
2. Heading "Networks and neuroscience" (pp. 165-166) describes what some call "principled heuristics". That is only simulating neurons to the granularity necessary to produce or account for the behavior. Such simplifications must not be arbitrary, but based on neuroscience coupled with behavior (i.e., must be principled).
3. Note that this book, Donahoe and Palmer (1994), uses many neuroscience terms with little or no definition or explanation. For more detail, see neuroscience references (and annotations) mentioned in [Overview: 04 - Neuroscience](../00%20-%20Overview/04%20-%20Neuroscience.md).

# Glossary

Donahoe, et al., publications in other articles and book chapters introduced me to the biobehavioral approach. In this glossary I am only listing terms with encountered in this Donahoe and Palmer (1994) book, with page references. 

Not all terms are strictly biobehavioral. Some terms are listed because I first encountered them in the works of Donahoe, et al. Some are included because Donahoe, et al., use the terms in an unfamiliar way. Some I just find interesting.

I will do the same within annotations for other works, and eventually create a master glossary.
