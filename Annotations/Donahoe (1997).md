# Annotations: The Necessity of Neural Networks (1997)

[Citation](../References.md#donahoe-1997)

---

This is chapter 1 of [*Neural-Network Models of Cognition: Biobehavioral Foundations*](../References.md#donahoe-1997). As such, it is both a high level summary of Donahoe's previous works, and an introduction/overview of the book. Most chapters are written from a neuroscience point of view.

If you read nothing else in this book, read this first chapter. 

1. Discusses Darwinian natural selection and the factors that eventually lead to the acceptance of it within the scientific community after about 70 years.
2. Draws parallels between evolution of notions of natural and behavioral selection.
3. Describes evolution of notion of behavioral selection, including:
	1. temporal contiguity
	2. behavioral discrepancy
4. Briefly describes the six major sections (pp 7-8):
	1. Neural development
	2. Neural plasticity
	3. Perceiving
	4. Behaving
	5. Reinforcement learning
	6. Complex behavior
5. Describes basic concepts and terminology of "Biological mechanisms of behavioral selection" (pp 8-11)
	1. Synaptic processes
	2. Neural systems
	3. Sensory and motor processes
	4. Behavioral constraints
	5. Formal techniques for tracing behavioral selection
6. "Interpreting Complex Behavior Using Neural Networks" describes critical components and functionality within SelNet. Most notably:
	1. Activation function (pp 13-14): description and equation for stimulation of network elements.
	2. Learning algorithm (pp 14-15): description and equation for changing the efficacy/strength of connections among elements.
7. "Neural networks and neuroscience": lengthy section covering several important concepts, 
	1. but this is perhaps the most important statement in the section: "Any neuroscience-based treatment of neural networks must yield results that are consistent with behavioral observations, and must satisfy standard criteria of parsimony and logical consistency."
	2. This ties back to [Skinner, B.F. (1938)](./References.md#skinner-1938) (page 454 in PDF, page 422 in hardcover?), Skinner noted **(emphasis mine)**:
		- "What is generally not understood by those interested in establishing neurological bases is that a ***rigorous description at the level of behavior is necessary*** for the demonstration of a neurological correlate.
