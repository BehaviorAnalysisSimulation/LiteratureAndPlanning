# Unified Principle of Reinforcement

Describes in great detail Donahoe's unified principle of reinforcment that treats respondent and operant conditioning as fundamentally the same in behavioral and biobehavioral terms, differing primarily in procedural terms.

"The environment acquires control over whatever responses occur in a favorable spatiotemporal relation to a homogeneous discrepancy (a discrepancy confined to the responses elicited by the reinforcer)." (pg 502)

"If a homogeneous discrepancy does not exist when the instrumental contingency is imposed, the unified reinforcement principle predicts that the occurrence of an otherwise effective reinforcing stimulus after the instrumental response will not produce conditioning." (pg 502)

Regarding the matching law, the topic of the book in which this chapter appears, "Matching is not a fundamental principle from the perspective of a unified principle of reinforcement; it is an important derivative relation found only under certain conditions." (pg 502)

Also see section "Concluding Comments" (pp 515-516).
# Usefulness?

1. The unified principle of reinforcement is the basis of Donahoe's bioplausible artificial neural network of future works. At some point the network is referred to as a "selectionist adaptive neural network", or just "selection network", and is nicknamed "SelNet". See:
	1. [Donahoe and Palmer (1989)](../References.md#donahoe-palmer-1989): Introduces the general structure and operation of the network (without naming it) in the context of comments on differences between a behavioral approach to complex behavior and that of cognitive psychology.
	2. [Donahoe, Burgos, and Palmer (1993)](../References.md#donahoe-burgos-palmer-1993): This ***may*** be Donahoe's first use of "biobehavioral" ("Toward a Biobehavioral Principle of Reinforcement", pg 19), as embodied in SelNet. Provides more details on structure and functioning, especially how operant and respondent conditioning work at a low level.
